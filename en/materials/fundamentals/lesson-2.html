Lesson 2: History, Generations & Types of Computers

Reading time: 15 minutes | Level: Beginner

Generations of Computers

GenerationPeriodTechnologyKey Features
1st Generation1940–1956Vacuum TubesLarge size, high power consumption, machine language, e.g. ENIAC, UNIVAC
2nd Generation1956–1963TransistorsSmaller, faster, assembly language, COBOL & FORTRAN, less heat
3rd Generation1964–1971Integrated Circuits (ICs)Even smaller, keyboards & monitors introduced, operating systems, multiprogramming
4th Generation1971–PresentMicroprocessorsPersonal computers (PCs), affordable, GUI, internet era
5th GenerationPresent & BeyondArtificial IntelligenceAI, machine learning, natural language processing, robotics

Types of Computers (By Size)

TypeDescriptionUse
SupercomputersMost powerful, fastest, complex calculationsWeather forecasting, nuclear research, space
Mainframe ComputersLarge, powerful, supports thousands of usersBanks, insurance, government data processing
MinicomputersMid-range, supports multiple usersEngineering, scientific computations
Microcomputers (PCs)Personal use, most common typeDesktops, laptops, tablets, smartphones
WorkstationsHigh-performance PCs for technical work3D design, video editing, CAD

Classification by Purpose

  • General-Purpose Computers: Versatile machines for word processing, browsing, gaming, programming (e.g., your PC)
  • Special-Purpose Computers: Designed for specific tasks only (e.g., ATM machines, washing machine controllers, traffic lights)

Classification by Function

  • Analog Computers: Process continuous data like temperature, speed (used in scientific/engineering applications)
  • Digital Computers: Process discrete binary data (0s and 1s) — most common type today
  • Hybrid Computers: Combine both analog and digital features (e.g., hospital ICU monitoring systems)
Key Points to Remember
  • 5 generations: Vacuum Tubes → Transistors → ICs → Microprocessors → AI
  • Types by size: Supercomputer > Mainframe > Mini > Micro (PC)
  • By purpose: General-purpose vs Special-purpose
  • By function: Analog, Digital, Hybrid
